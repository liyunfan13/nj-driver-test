Right of way and intersections

The law tells you who must yield. It never gives you a right to hit someone. When in doubt, yield.

Pedestrians

  • Stop and stay stopped for a pedestrian in a marked crosswalk. This applies when the pedestrian is on your half of the road, or within one lane of it.
  • Yield to pedestrians at every intersection, including intersections with no crosswalk lines.
  • Never pass a car that is stopped at a crosswalk.
  • Do not stop with any part of your car in the crosswalk.
  • A pedestrian who crosses on a green or “Walk” signal has the right of way over turning cars.
  • Penalty for failure to yield: $200 fine, 2 points, and up to 15 days of community service.

Emergency vehicles

  • When you see flashing red or blue lights or hear a siren, pull to the right edge of the road and stop.
  • Stay stopped until the emergency vehicle passes.
  • Move Over law: When you approach any stopped vehicle with flashing or blinking lights, move to a lane that is not next to it. If you cannot move over safely, slow down below the speed limit and be ready to stop. The fine is $100 to $500. Give the same space to a vehicle marked with flares or warning triangles. The law does not name those, but it is the safe thing to do.

Intersections

  • At a four-way stop, the first car to stop goes first.
  • If two cars stop at the same time, the car on the left yields to the car on the right.
  • When you turn left, yield to oncoming traffic and pedestrians.
  • When you enter a road from a driveway, alley, or parking lot, stop and yield to all traffic and pedestrians.
  • Do not enter an intersection if you cannot get all the way through it.

New Jersey special turns

  • Jughandle: Many New Jersey highways have no left-turn lanes. To turn left, exit to the right onto a ramp. The ramp loops you around to cross the highway.
  • Right turn on red: You may turn right on red after a full stop, unless a sign says no. Yield to pedestrians and traffic first.
  • Traffic circles: Watch the signs. Yield when a yield sign tells you to.

Road lines

  • Yellow lines separate traffic that moves in opposite directions.
  • White lines separate lanes that move in the same direction.
  • A solid centerline on your side means do not pass.
  • A broken line on your side means you may pass when it is safe.
  • Double solid yellow means no passing from either side.

Railroad crossings

  • Stop when lights flash, a gate is down, or a train comes.
  • Never go around a lowered gate.
  • Do not shift gears while you cross the tracks.

Alcohol, drugs and DWI

New Jersey calls drunk or drugged driving DWI (driving while intoxicated).

BAC limits

DriverIllegal BAC
Age 21 or older0.08% or higher
Commercial driver in a commercial vehicle0.04% or higher
Under age 210.01% or higher (zero tolerance)

You can also get a DWI with a lower BAC if alcohol or drugs make you an unsafe driver.

Implied consent

When you drive in New Jersey, you agree to take a breath test if police arrest you for DWI. If you refuse, you get a separate charge with its own penalties.

First-offense DWI penalties (age 21 or older)

BACFineIgnition interlock
0.08% to under 0.10%$250 to $4003 months
0.10% to under 0.15%$300 to $5007 to 12 months
0.15% or higher$300 to $5003 months of license forfeiture with interlock, then interlock for 12 to 15 more months

All first offenders can also get up to 30 days in jail, 12 to 48 hours at an Intoxicated Driver Resource Center (IDRC), and a $1,000 surcharge each year for 3 years. A third offense brings a $1,000 fine, 180 days in jail, and a long license suspension.

Facts about alcohol

  • These drinks hold the same amount of alcohol: one 12-ounce beer, one 5-ounce glass of wine, and one 1.5-ounce shot of liquor.
  • Only time makes you sober. Coffee, cold showers, and fresh air do not.
  • Alcohol slows your reaction time and hurts your judgment before you feel drunk.
  • Signs of a drunk driver: weaving between lanes, driving much slower than traffic, and sudden stops.
  • If you see a drunk driver, keep your distance and call the police when it is safe.

GDL rules (permit and probationary license)

New Jersey uses a Graduated Driver License (GDL) system. These rules apply to permit holders and probationary drivers under age 21.

The steps

  1. Get a permit. You can get a special learner’s permit at 16 if you take a behind-the-wheel course.
  2. Pass the knowledge and vision tests. The MVC then validates your permit.
  3. Practice with a supervising driver. Drivers under 21 practice for 6 months. Drivers 21 or older practice for 3 months.
  4. Log your hours. Drivers under 21 need at least 50 practice hours, with 10 hours in darkness. A parent or supervising driver signs form BA-CSD.
  5. Pass the road test. If you fail, you can test again in 14 days.
  6. Drive on a probationary license for 1 year. Then upgrade to a basic license.

You have 2 years from the date of your permit to pass the tests and get your probationary license. The probationary year comes after that.

Supervising driver

The supervising driver sits in the front seat. This person must be at least 21, hold a valid New Jersey license, and have at least 3 years of driving experience.

Point system and penalties

The MVC adds points to your record for each moving violation. More points bring surcharges and then a suspension.

Point limits and ways to lose points

PointsResult
6 or more in 3 years$150 surcharge, plus $25 for each point over 6. The MVC checks again each year as old points age out.
12 or moreLicense suspension
  • Drive 1 full year with no violations and no suspension: the MVC removes 3 points.
  • Finish an MVC-approved defensive driving course: the MVC removes 2 points. You can do this once every 5 years. Neither credit lowers the points the MVC uses for surcharges.

Safe driving

School buses

  • When a school bus shows flashing red lights, stop at least 25 feet away. Stay stopped until the lights go off and the children are safe.
  • This rule applies from both directions on a two-lane road.
  • On a divided highway with a median or safety island, traffic on the other side slows to 10 mph or less.
  • When a bus is parked at the curb at a school, loading or unloading children on that side of the street, you may pass at 10 mph or less. Otherwise, the 25-foot stop rule applies.

Frozen dessert (ice cream) trucks

  • When the truck shows flashing red lights or a stop arm, stop.
  • Then pass slowly, at no more than 15 mph, and watch for children.
  • You do not need to stop on a dual highway when a median separates you from the truck.
  • Always stop for a person who crosses the road to or from the truck.

Seat belts and child seats

  • Everyone in the car must wear a seat belt, front and back.
  • Children under 2 and under 30 pounds ride in a rear-facing seat with a five-point harness.
  • Children under 4 and under 40 pounds ride in a harness seat (rear-facing or forward-facing).
  • Children under 8 and under 57 inches ride in a harness seat or a booster seat, in the rear seat.
  • Never put a rear-facing seat in front of an active airbag.

Following distance and speed

  • Keep at least 3 seconds between you and the car ahead. Add time in rain, snow, or fog.
  • Slow down before a curve, not in it.
  • Slow down on narrow or winding roads, at intersections and railroad crossings, and on wet roads.
  • At night, drive at a speed that lets you stop within the distance your headlights show.
  • Stopping distance depends on reaction time, brakes, tires, speed, and the road surface. It does not depend on the time of day.

Passing

  • Pass on the left. You may pass on the right only when the road has two or more lanes in your direction, or when the car ahead turns left and there is room. Never leave the pavement to pass.
  • Do not pass on hills, on curves, at intersections, or at railroad crossings.
  • When someone passes you, keep right and do not speed up.
  • Keep right on all roads except to pass.

Emergencies

  • Skid: Take your foot off the gas. Steer in the direction you want the front of the car to go.
  • Brake failure: Pump the brake pedal. Use the parking brake slowly. Shift to a lower gear.
  • Tire blowout: Hold the wheel firmly. Ease off the gas. Do not brake hard.
  • Wipers stop in rain or snow: Slow down, turn on your hazard lights, and pull off the road.
  • Collision you cannot avoid: Hit something that will give way rather than something rigid. Hit something still rather than something that moves toward you. Try for a glancing blow.
  • Car goes into water: Open a window and get out fast.

Sharing the road

  • When you pass bicycles, pedestrians, or scooters, move over a lane if you can. If not, give at least 4 feet. If neither is safe, slow to 25 mph or less and be ready to stop.
  • Trucks have large blind spots (“No-Zones”). If you cannot see the truck driver in the truck’s mirror, the driver cannot see you.
  • Watch for motorcycles. They are small and hard to see.

What to carry when you drive

Keep your driver license (or permit), the vehicle registration, and the insurance card with you.

Test-day tips

  1. Study the numbers tables in these notes on the day before the test.
  2. Bring your permit and all your 6 Points of ID documents.
  3. Bring your glasses or contact lenses for the vision test.
  4. Read each question two times. Watch for words such as “not”, “never”, and “always”.
  5. When two answers look right, check for “all of the above”. It is often correct on this test.
  6. Skip a hard question and come back to it.
  7. If you fail, study the topics you missed and test again after 7 days.

Laws change. Check the current Driver Manual before your test. The test questions come from it.
